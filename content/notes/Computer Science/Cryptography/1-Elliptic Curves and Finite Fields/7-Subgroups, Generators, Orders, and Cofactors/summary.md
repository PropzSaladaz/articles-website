**Core analogy:** Treat the finite curve group as a circular route.

- **Curve order $\#E$:** total number of stops in the complete group.
- **Point order $\operatorname{ord}(P)$:** lap length—the first positive $n$ with $nP=\mathcal{O}$.
- **Generated subgroup $\langle P\rangle$:** all stops reachable by repeatedly taking the $P$-step.
- **Generator:** a point whose multiples reach every element of the group being discussed.
- **Base point $G$:** the public generator selected by a parameter set.
- **Subgroup order:** $n=\operatorname{ord}(G)$. When the selected order is prime, pairing literature commonly names it $r$.
- **Cofactor $h$:** number of subgroup-sized cosets in the full group:
  $$
  \#E=hn,
  \qquad
  h=\frac{\#E}{n}.
  $$

The notation $a\mid b$ means “$a$ divides $b$,” so $b=ca$ for some integer $c$. Lagrange's theorem guarantees that a point's order divides the curve order:

$$
\operatorname{ord}(P)\mid\#E(\mathbb{F}_p).
$$

## Running example

$P=(1,5)$ takes a 14-stop lap and generates the whole curve. Choosing $G=2P=(3,3)$ skips every other stop, producing a prime-order subgroup with

$$
\#E=14,
\qquad
n=r=7,
\qquad
h=2.
$$

## Base field and scalar field

- The curve coefficients and point coordinates live in the **base field** $\mathbb{F}_p$. Here, $x_G,y_G\in\mathbb{F}_{11}$.
- The point $G$ lives in the subgroup $\langle G\rangle\subset E(\mathbb{F}_{11})$; it is not itself a field element.
- Scalars live in the **scalar field** $\mathbb{F}_r$. Here, $k\in\mathbb{F}_7$.
- Scalar multiplication maps a scalar and a subgroup point back to the subgroup:
  $$
  k\in\mathbb{F}_r,
  \quad
  G\in\langle G\rangle,
  \quad
  kG\in\langle G\rangle.
  $$
- Scalars wrap around modulo $r$ because $rG=\mathcal{O}$, so $(k+r)G=kG$.

**Protocol bridge:** A nonzero private scalar $k\in\mathbb{F}_r$ produces the public point $Q=kG$; production systems make $r$ enormous.
