**Core analogy:** Treat the finite curve group as a circular route.

- **Curve order $\#E$:** total number of stops in the complete group.
- **Point order $\operatorname{ord}(P)$:** lap length—the first positive $n$ with $nP=\mathcal{O}$.
- **Generated subgroup $\langle P\rangle$:** all stops reachable by repeatedly taking the $P$-step.
- **Generator:** a point whose multiples reach every element of the group being discussed.
- **Base point $G$:** the public generator selected by a parameter set.
- **Subgroup order $n$:** $n=\operatorname{ord}(G)$.
- **Cofactor $h$:** number of subgroup-sized cosets in the full group:
  $$
  \#E=hn,
  \qquad
  h=\frac{\#E}{n}.
  $$

Lagrange's theorem guarantees that a point's order divides the curve order.

## Running example

$P=(1,5)$ takes a 14-stop lap and generates the whole curve. Choosing $G=2P=(3,3)$ skips every other stop, producing a prime-order subgroup with

$$
\#E=14,
\qquad
n=7,
\qquad
h=2.
$$
